## Who I am and what this is

I'm an A-level student building my EPQ, the **Silicon Neuron Project**: a spiking
neural network (SNN) and a standard ANN both classify MNIST digits on a Raspberry
Pi Pico, and I measure which uses less energy. The demo is a black foam-board box
with a **Pepper's-ghost hologram**: a 7-inch screen lies face down in the roof, a
2 mm acrylic sheet at 45° reflects it, and the live spikes, membrane voltages and
measured energy appear to float in mid-air in front of a mint tin holding the Pico.

I will build the physical box myself, so I need a **rendered 3D animated tutorial
in Blender**, like a LEGO instruction booklet: each part flies into place one step
at a time, with a numbered caption, and I can scrub backwards to any step (for
example, to see exactly where the breadboard and diode go).

This folder already contains working code from an earlier session. Your job is to
take it from "working draft" to a finished, verified tutorial. Read the files
before changing anything; do not rewrite from scratch.

## Fixed design facts (do not change without asking me)

Coordinates: millimetres, x to the viewer's right, y away from the viewer
(front opening at y = 0), z up from the table.

**Enclosure** — 5 mm black foam board. Interior 196 W × 220 D; viewing chamber
120 mm tall; 45 mm electronics cap above the roof. Outside ≈ 206 × 225 × 180 mm.
- Floor 196 × 220. Sides ×2 225 × 180. Back 196 × 180 (24 × 12 cable slot top-left).
- Roof 196 × 220, underside 125 mm above the table, window 154.2 × 85.9 for the
  screen's lit area, Ø14 wire hole 26 mm from the right and back edges.
- Valance 196 × 8 under the roof's front edge. Cap front 196 × 45 with Ø24 button
  hole 30 mm from the right. Cap lid 196 × 220 with 6 vent slots.
- Pane rails ×4, 170 × 8, glued in pairs 3 mm apart on each side wall along a 45° line.

**Optics** — acrylic 190 × 170 × 2 mm at 45°, foot at y = 20 on the floor, top
meeting the roof 140 mm back. Reflection in the plane y − z = c maps a screen
point (y₀, z_screen) to (z_screen + c, y₀ − c), so the image stands on the
vertical **ghost plane y = 140 mm**. The screen's lit area has its back edge
directly above the pane's top. Image is mirrored left-right in software.

**Electronics**
- 7" 1024×600 IPS HDMI screen (outline 164.9 × 100 × 5, lit area 154.2 × 85.9,
  driver board 110 × 60 on its back), face down over the roof window.
- Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W (65 × 30) and INA226 module (≈36 × 21, 0.1 Ω shunt) taped
  to the roof behind the screen, inside the cap: Pi on the left, meter on the right.
- 24 mm arcade button in the cap front.
- **Mint tin** (95 × 61 × 21, lid hinged at the back, open ~95°) centred about
  150 mm behind the front opening, under the ghost plane.
- **Half-size 400-point breadboard** (82.5 × 54.5 × 8.5) filling the tin: rows 1–30
  along x, columns a–e front / f–j back, power rails along both long sides, red
  (+) rail at the BACK.
- **Raspberry Pi Pico** pressed into rows 1–20, USB end LEFT, pins in columns c
  (pins 1–20) and h (pins 40–21), so Pico pin 1 = row 1, column c.
- **1N5819 Schottky diode**: plain end (anode) in the back red + rail at row 6,
  banded end (cathode) in hole j2 = same strip as Pico pin 39 (VSYS).

**Wiring** (13 jumpers, no soldering)
| From | To | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Pi pin 2 (5 V) | INA226 VIN+ | red |
| INA226 VIN− | breadboard back + rail (→ diode → VSYS) | red |
| Pi pin 1 (3.3 V) | INA226 VCC | orange |
| Pi pin 3 (SDA) | INA226 SDA | white |
| Pi pin 5 (SCL) | INA226 SCL | grey |
| Pi pin 6 (GND) | INA226 GND | black |
| Pi pin 8 (TXD) | hole a2 = Pico pin 2 (GP1, UART RX) | yellow |
| Pi pin 10 (RXD) | hole a1 = Pico pin 1 (GP0, UART TX) | green |
| Pi pin 11 (GPIO17) | hole a4 = Pico pin 4 (GP2, marker) | blue |
| Pi pin 9 (GND) | hole a3 = Pico pin 3 (GND) | black |
| Pi pin 13 (GPIO27) / pin 14 (GND) | button legs | purple / black |
| Pi mini-HDMI / USB via OTG | screen HDMI / screen USB | cable |

The five tin wires go up through the roof's Ø14 hole. The Pico's 5 V must reach it
**only** through the INA226 (never straight from the Pi).

## The assembly order (22 steps; the build order is physically checked — keep it)

0. Cover — the finished, powered demo with the hologram showing.
1. Kit — every part exploded and labelled.
2. Mint tin (sub-assembly built on the table beside the box).
3. Half-size breadboard into the tin.
4. Pico into rows 1–20 (flash the .uf2 first, then unplug USB).
5. 1N5819: anode in back + rail row 6, cathode in j2.
6. Floor. 7. Side walls. 8. Back wall.
9. Place the Pico tin — **before** the pane, because afterwards it is unreachable;
   plug the five jumpers into the + rail and a1–a4 now, loose ends gathered at the back right.
10. Pane rails. 11. Acrylic pane. 12. Roof (feed the five loose jumpers up through the hole).
13. Valance. 14. Screen face down. 15. Pi Zero 2 W and INA226.
16. Meter wires. 17. Screen cables. 18. Pico wires (right wall hidden as a cut-away).
19. Button and cap front. 20. Lid and USB power. 21. Power on: screen lights, hologram appears.

## How to work

1. Run `python blender/build_demo.py` and `python blender/assembly_animation.py`
   (or `blender -b -P blender/assembly_animation.py`) and fix anything that errors
   on this Blender version. The scripts were developed against the pip `bpy` 5.0
   module; Blender 4.2–4.x differs in a few APIs (e.g. `surface_render_method`
   vs `blend_method`, layered actions vs `action.fcurves`) — keep both paths.
2. **Before rendering everything, render one test frame per step** (the settled
   frame ≈ step start + 80), put them in a contact sheet, and look at it. Check:
   - every part arriving in that step is in frame and not hidden behind a wall;
   - the step's part is visible against its background (Workbench draws metallic
     materials near-black — metallic is set to 0 for that reason);
   - captions never cover the part being placed (move the camera if they do);
   - nothing appears before its step or disappears after it;
   - the diode, Pico pins and breadboard holes are readable in steps 3–5 close-ups;
   - wires grow along their routes and end in the right holes (step 16 and 18).
   Fix and re-check before the full render.
3. Full render: `python blender/assembly_animation.py --render`. At 1280×720
   Workbench takes ≈2 s per frame on a laptop CPU (~70 min for 2,112 frames).
   Frames already rendered are skipped, so it can resume. If time matters, add
   `--every=2` for a 12 fps draft first.
4. `make_video.py` runs automatically at the end; run it alone to redo captions.
5. Open the MP4 and the PDF, step through every chapter, and confirm each one
   matches the step list above. Report anything you could not verify.

## Things that went wrong last time (avoid them)

- Two different animation scripts ran at once and overwrote each other's frames.
  There is exactly **one** animation script: `assembly_animation.py`. Do not create
  another; never run two renders into the same folder.
- `pkill -f <pattern>` killed my own shell when the pattern appeared in the
  command line. Kill renders by PID.
- `obj.matrix_world = …` after re-parenting needs a depsgraph update; every rig
  sits at the origin, so parenting without changing `matrix_world` is enough.
- Workbench cannot render textures through transparency, which is why the ghost
  image is composited in `make_video.py` from `ghost_quads.json`. Keep that path.
- Earlier versions had the tin placed after the pane (impossible to build), a
  170-point breadboard the Pico didn't fit into, and the Pico's 5 V wired straight
  from the Pi (bypassing the meter). All three are fixed — don't reintroduce them.

## Style of the tutorial

Clear, neutral background; dark foam board drawn mid-grey so it reads; part
outlines on; one part moving at a time; camera eases to each step's viewpoint in
the first 16 frames, then holds still while the part flies in (frames 14–62 of
the step) and settles. Captions: yellow tile with the step number, bold title,
one or two lines of plain instructions, progress dots along the bottom. Short,
literal wording: what to pick up, where it goes, which way round.
